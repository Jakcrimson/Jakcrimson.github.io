A year after the [WAIRO proposal]({{ '/posts/wairo-proposal/' | relative_url }}),
the people building frontier AI systems started saying much the same thing. This
essay takes that as its starting point.

It works through four things: what researchers inside the frontier labs have
said publicly about pacing and alignment; why the risks are no longer
hypothetical, taking the reward-hacking incident OpenAI disclosed in July 2026
as the example; what legislatures actually did this year, from the EU's Digital
Omnibus deferral to Executive Order 14365 and the money now being spent on
state-level pre-emption in the US; and why the variables that should decide who
writes these rules are technical fluency, time horizon and who bears the
consequences — not age.

It closes on four concrete asks: binding synchronised pacing rules,
early-career seats on scientific advisory bodies, mandatory independent
technical testimony before votes, and legal protection for researchers who
speak.
